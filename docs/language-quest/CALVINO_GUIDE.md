# The Calvino guide: one set of lenses for every language's city

Purpose (owner request): use the thematic structure of Italo Calvino's _Invisible Cities_ as a **guide across languages**, for the
style and the description of the cities. Whatever language the player chooses, the city is seen through the same family of lenses, so
the player moving from one language to another recognises the _way of looking_ even though the place, words and history change.

## 1. What the book does (checked against secondary sources)

From the sources I could reach by search (not the book itself; [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invisible_Cities) and
[EBSCO Research Starters](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/literature-and-writing/invisible-cities-italo-calvino/) were in the results, and I could not open them for the full text):

- The frame is a conversation: **Marco Polo describes cities to Kublai Khan**.
- There are **55 cities, all with women's names**, in **eleven thematic groups of five**, spread over **nine chapters** in a rigid, patterned order.
- The eleven groups: **Memory, Desire, Signs, Thin, Trading, Eyes, Names, the Dead, the Sky, Continuous, Hidden.**
- Examples from the results: _Zaira_ (Memory: the present city contains all its past, written in corners, window gratings and banisters); _Zenobia_ (Thin: pilings and ladders, built in a dry place as if on water); _Anastasia_ (Desire: abundance as a cage).

_Correction to earlier drafts:_ my first list omitted the **Thin** group. Please check the final list and exact group titles against the text before publishing.

## 2. How the game uses it

1. **Each chapter is an "invisible city"** seen through one lens. The lens, not the era, is its organising idea; the epoch and the myth serve it.
2. **The same eleven lenses are available to every language.** A new language picks its own chapters but draws from the same list; two languages can describe different cities with the same lens, and the notebook's cross-language tab ([OPEN_WORLD_AND_QUESTS.md](./OPEN_WORLD_AND_QUESTS.md)) can link them.
3. **Each chapter opens with a short city card** in the Marco Polo voice (English; the language of description is the game's interface language), titled by **one word of the target language** that the player will learn in that chapter.
4. The card is **not a lesson**. It is a lens: it should make the player look at the place differently, and it must not state any word's meaning (pillar 1).

## 3. Style rules for a city card

1. **Voice:** a traveller reporting to a ruler who has not been there. Calm, declarative, precise, slightly amused.
2. **Length:** 60 to 120 words. One paragraph.
3. **One rule:** the city obeys a single principle derived from its lens (it forgets nothing; it rebuilds itself from memory; everything is named for what it no longer does).
4. **One impossible detail, rendered exactly:** a concrete image that could not exist and is described as if it did.
5. **One turn:** the last sentence quietly reverses or complicates the first (the arrows lead where earlier travellers _thought_ the exit was).
6. **No answers:** no target-language meaning, no teaching, no moral stated outright.
7. **Wes Anderson register on the page:** deadpan, tiny absurd particulars, tableau-like.
8. **Original text only.** Do not quote or closely paraphrase Calvino.

## 5. Cross-language use

| Idea                        | How it works                                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| --------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Same lens, different city   | In a Japanese or Portuguese world (for example), chapter 2 is still a **Names** city, built from that country's own naming habits                                                                                                |
| The title word              | Each card is titled by one word of the target language, learned in that chapter; the word's meaning is a clue to the lens only after the player has earned it                                                                    |
| Cognates as invisible links | Where theme words are cognates across the languages the player has studied, the cross-language notebook can connect them (for example Danish _navn_, English _name_, German _Name_; I believe these are cognates, to be sourced) |
| Pack data                   | A chapter declares `lens` and a `card`; both are content, the engine only displays them (no change to the dynamics)                                                                                                              |
| Chapter shape               | 5 encounters per chapter, five cities per lens in the book: a chapter may add up to four optional "cities" (side quests) around its main one, echoing the groups of five                                                         |
