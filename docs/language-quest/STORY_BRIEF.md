# Language Quest: story brief (one page)

Use this as the reference when writing a story. The full concept is in [GAME_DESIGN_DOCUMENT.md](./GAME_DESIGN_DOCUMENT.md).

## The idea in three lines
You are a quiet visitor in a place whose language you don't speak. People around you are busy with ordinary things and
nobody explains. By watching and listening you work out what the words mean; at the end you speak one sentence that
proves it, and the story's last door opens.

## Rules a story must obey
1. **Nobody translates.** No narrator, no subtitles. Meaning comes from what you can see people do.
2. **Ordinary stakes.** Fetch water, serve coffee, open a gate. Small, physical, readable. No chosen ones.
3. **One chain.** Five or so encounters, each unlocking the next. Each is one place and one person.
4. **Every word is earned twice**: heard, then seen in at least two different kinds of clue (an object, an action, a gesture, writing, a contrast, a context).
5. **Include noise.** At least one clue per scene that fits nothing, like real life.
6. **Grammar by repetition with a change**: one cup / two cups; the same sentence with and without "not"; two halves joined by "because". Never named in the scene.
7. **The last scene reuses everything** in a sentence the player has not heard whole.
8. **Calm tone.** Nothing is lost by guessing wrong; the truth arrives at the end.

## Shape of a chapter (about 25 minutes)
| Beat | Purpose | Pilot example (Danish, Nyhavn) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 Observe | One concrete noun made obvious by an action | A child drinks at the pump: *vand* |
| 2 Connect | Link a noun to a count or pattern | One cup, many cups: *kop, kopper* |
| 3 Hear | Contrast reveals a function word | The harbour master with and without a key: *jeg har (ikke) en nøgle* |
| 4 Compose | A reason joins two ideas | The gate is shut, he needs a key, *fordi* |
| 5 Speak | Prove it in your own sentence | The archivist waits: *Jeg har brug for en nøgle, fordi porten er lukket.* |

The pilot's thread is simple: a locked harbour gate, a key, and an archivist who opens the last door when you say why.

## Place and look
Pick a real city district whose colours people recognise; soften those colours to pastel. The place is a small stack of
floating terraces joined by stairs: the story should climb (start on the quay, end on the highest terrace at a door or a gate).
Tall buildings only at the back; low furniture in front.

## Characters (keep to 5)
One person per encounter, each with a prop that does the teaching: a child with a cup, a vendor with cups, a harbour
master with a key, a traveller without one, an archivist with folded arms. Name roles, not backstories.

## Ask yourself before writing
Can a stranger guess each word from the scene alone, but only after noticing two things? Does any line give the answer
away? Could the same scene mislead in a fair way? Would the final sentence make sense to the person at the door?

## Honesty note
If the language is real, have the spellings, transcriptions and example sentences checked by a native speaker before
publishing; until then mark them unverified.
